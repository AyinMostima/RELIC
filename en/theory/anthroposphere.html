Anthroposphere Theory

Scope, process inventory, and fail-closed boundary status

The RELIC anthroposphere domain (relic.scientific.anthroposphere) governs anthropogenic emission sector splitting and chemical-mechanism speciation, continuous-time Markov land-use fraction transitions and area-weighted land-use parameter observations, multi-resource management coordination, and agricultural or disturbance interventions (irrigation, fertilization, harvest, livestock grazing, prescribed/wildland fire, and tillage) (Methods of Ecological Analysis, Chapter 24).

Fourteen classes in relic.scientific.anthroposphere carry declarative IPHSEquivalenceCertificate files. RELIC enforces a strict distinction between the eleven implemented ([I]) scientific Functors (2 at Level 1, 3 at Level 2, and 6 at Level 3) and the three fail-closed ([N], Level 0) boundary placeholders whose runtime interconnection contracts remain unbound:

Because anthropogenic harvesting, irrigation withdrawal, fertilizer import, and industrial emissions are externally driven open-system interventions, no anthroposphere class claims Level 4 or Level 5 closed thermodynamic GENERIC structure. General ledger conventions follow Mathematical Foundations and structural classifications follow DEC, IPHS, and GENERIC.

Fail-closed Level-0 boundaries vs implemented emission operators

Fail-closed boundary classes ([N], Level 0)

EmissionInventory, AtmosphereCoupling, and AnthroposphereInterfaceMapping are registered in the governance catalog at Level 0 with empty runtime port bindings (implementation_status = "not_implemented_fail_closed"). Instantiating or invoking forward() on any of these three classes raises an explicit `[N]` runtime_dependency_unbound error without fabricating proxy weights, plume-rise heights, or silent zero fluxes. Preprocessed gridded emission fields therefore enter active workflows as validated external forcings consumed directly by SectorAllocation and SpeciesMapper.

Conservative sector allocation (SectorAllocation, Level 1)

For species \(s\) and grid support \(i\) with total anthropogenic mass flux \(E_{s,i}\) [kg m\(^{-2}\) s\(^{-1}\)], sector priors \(\pi_{q,s} \ge 0\), and spatial activity proxies \(P_{q,i} \ge 0\) (Crippa et al., 2020; McDuffie et al., 2020), relic.scientific.anthroposphere.emissions.sector_allocation.SectorAllocation normalizes sector weights pointwise:

\[ W_{q,s,i} = \frac{\pi_{q,s}\,P_{q,i}}{\sum_r \pi_{r,s}\,P_{r,i}}, \qquad E_{q,s,i} = W_{q,s,i}\,E_{s,i}, \qquad R_{s,i} = E_{s,i} - \sum_q E_{q,s,i}. \]

Zero or non-finite normalization denominators fail closed without defaulting to uniform sector shares, and \(R_{s,i}\) audits floating-point partition closure.

Mechanism speciation: molar gas vs mass aerosol basis (SpeciesMapper, Level 3)

relic.scientific.anthroposphere.emissions.species_mapping.SpeciesMapper projects sector-resolved inventory mass-flux vectors \(\mathbf{u}_q\) [kg m\(^{-2}\) s\(^{-1}\)] onto target chemical-mechanism species (such as CB05 gas lumping plus primary aerosol classes; Seinfeld and Pandis, 2016) while keeping gas-phase molar fluxes and aerosol mass fluxes on separate typed channels:

\[ \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{gas,mol}} = \star_{\mathrm{MW}^{-1}}\,S_{\mathrm{gas}}\,\mathbf{u}_q \quad [\mathrm{mol\,m^{-2}\,s^{-1}}], \qquad \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{aer,mass}} = S_{\mathrm{aer}}\,\mathbf{u}_q \quad [\mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}\,s^{-1}}], \]

where \(S_{\mathrm{gas}}\) and \(S_{\mathrm{aer}}\) hold non-negative sector split fractions and \(\star_{\mathrm{MW}^{-1}}\) divides each gas channel by its SSOT-backed molecular weight. Any inventory mass fraction not assigned to active mechanism species is routed explicitly to \(\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{unmapped}}\) so the mapping residual

\[ \mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{map}} = \mathbf{u}_q - S_{\mathrm{covered}}\,\mathbf{u}_q - \mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{unmapped}} \]

vanishes within configured closure tolerance. Gas molar fluxes and aerosol mass fluxes are never summed across incompatible units.

Continuous-time Markov land-use transitions and parameter projection

relic.scientific.anthroposphere.land_use.transitions.LandUseTransitions (Level 3) evolves the nine-class land-use area-fraction row vector \(\mathbf{p}(t) \in \Delta^8\) (\(\mathbf{p}\ge \mathbf{0}\), \(\mathbf{p}\mathbf{1} = 1\); Hurtt et al., 2020) via a continuous-time Markov generator matrix \(Q\) [yr\(^{-1}\)] satisfying \(Q_{ij} \ge 0\) for \(i \neq j\) and zero row sums \(Q\mathbf{1} = \mathbf{0}\):

\[ \mathbf{p}(t + \Delta t) = \mathbf{p}(t)\,\exp\!\left(Q\,\Delta t_{\mathrm{yr}}\right). \]

Because \(Q\mathbf{1} = \mathbf{0}\) and off-diagonal rates are non-negative, the matrix exponential \(\exp(Q\,\Delta t_{\mathrm{yr}})\) is row-stochastic for every \(\Delta t_{\mathrm{yr}} \ge 0\), preserving simplex non-negativity and exact total-area conservation \(\mathbf{p}(t + \Delta t)\mathbf{1} = \mathbf{p}(t)\mathbf{1} = 1\) in a single REPLACE step update. Optional socio-economic rate scaling modulates \(Q_{ij} = s'\,Q_{ij}^{\mathrm{base}}\) (\(i \neq j\)) while recomputing \(Q_{ii} = -\sum_{j \neq i} Q_{ij}\) so row-sum degeneracy is never broken.

relic.scientific.anthroposphere.land_use.mapping.LandUseParameterMapping (Level 1, PhysicalFieldFunctor) computes read-only area-weighted surface properties \(\overline{\theta} = \sum_{i=1}^{9} p_i \theta_i\) (REPLACE) without mutating vegetation, soil, or hydrological prognostic stores.

Management coordination and agricultural water-nutrient ports

Multi-resource management coordination (ManagementCoordinator, Level 3)

relic.scientific.anthroposphere.management.coordinator.ManagementCoordinator observes natural state deficits and management flux requests to publish normalized priority weights \(p_i = q_i / \sum_j q_j\) (\(q_i = w_i(1 + s_i)\), \(w_i \ge 0\)) and dimensionless resource-availability scaling \(\eta \in [0, 1]\) from unit-normalized demand ratios \(c = \sum_r R_r / Q_r\). Water, labor, fuel, and machinery quantities are normalized by their respective quota bases \(Q_r\) before aggregation and are never added across unlike physical dimensions; ManagementCoordinator never invokes child management Functors directly.

Irrigation withdrawal, conveyance efficiency, and WEAP21 quota rationing (IrrigationManagement, Level 2)

relic.scientific.anthroposphere.management.irrigation.IrrigationManagement (Level 2, TransportMechanismFunctor) evaluates demand-triggered agricultural water withdrawal on irrigated cropland cells (\(c_{\mathrm{lu},i} = 1\)) when volumetric soil moisture \(\theta_i\) falls below \(\theta_{\mathrm{trig},i} = \theta_{\mathrm{wp},i} + f_{\mathrm{trig}}(\theta_{\mathrm{fc},i} - \theta_{\mathrm{wp},i})\) (Allen et al., 1998). With root-zone depth \(Z_r\), liquid water density \(\rho_w\) (get_physical_constant("water_density")), precipitation mass flux \(F_{P,i}\), system efficiency \(\eta_i \in (0, 1]\) selected by irrigation_type (1: surface, 2: sprinkler, 3: drip, 4: subsurface), and maximum gross mass-flux rate \(F_{\max}\), pre-quota gross withdrawal mass \(A_i\) [kg m\(^{-2}\)] is

\[ M_{\mathrm{net},i} = \max\!\left[\max(\theta_{\mathrm{fc},i} - \theta_i, 0)\,Z_r \rho_w - F_{P,i}\,\Delta t,\; 0\right], \qquad A_i = u_i\,\min\!\left(\frac{M_{\mathrm{net},i}}{\eta_i},\; F_{\max}\,\Delta t\right), \]

where \(u_i = \mathbf{1}_{\theta_i < \theta_{\mathrm{trig},i}}\,\mathbf{1}_{c_{\mathrm{lu},i} = 1}\). Converting cell withdrawal to depth via \(h_w = 1000 / \rho_w\) [mm m\(^2\) kg\(^{-1}\)] yields total requested domain quota depth \(D = \sum_i A_i h_w\) [mm]. Against scalar irrigation_remaining_quota \(Q \ge 0\) [mm], single-priority WEAP21 equal-percentage rationing (Yates et al., 2005) scales all active cells proportionally by \(s \in [0, 1]\):

\[\begin{split} s = \begin{cases} 1, & D \le Q,\\ \dfrac{Q}{D}, & D > Q, \end{cases} \qquad M_{\mathrm{gross},i} = s\,A_i, \qquad M_{\mathrm{deliv},i} = \eta_i\,M_{\mathrm{gross},i}. \end{split}\]

Step fluxes \(F_{\mathrm{abs},i} = M_{\mathrm{gross},i}/\Delta t\) (abstraction_flux), \(F_{\mathrm{irr},i} = M_{\mathrm{deliv},i}/\Delta t\) (irrigation_flux), and depleted quota \(Q' = \max(Q - \sum_i M_{\mathrm{gross},i} h_w, 0)\) are emitted via REPLACE, enforcing the conveyance ledger \(\lvert F_{\mathrm{irr},i} - \eta_i F_{\mathrm{abs},i} \rvert \le \varepsilon_w\) without directly mutating soil_moisture.

Step-overlap-invariant fertilization (FertilizationManagement, Level 2)

relic.scientific.anthroposphere.management.fertilization.FertilizationManagement converts scheduled fertilizer product applications \(A_e\) [kg m\(^{-2}\)] of duration \(\tau_e\) with elemental nitrogen fraction \(f_{N,e}\) and labeled phosphate fraction \(f_{\mathrm{P_2O_5},e}\) into elemental mass inputs using SSOT atomic masses \(\chi_P = 2 M_P / (2 M_P + \frac{5}{2} M_{\mathrm{O_2}})\):

\[ \Delta M_N = \sum_e A_e f_{N,e}\,\frac{\omega_e}{\tau_e}, \qquad \Delta M_P = \sum_e A_e f_{\mathrm{P_2O_5},e}\chi_P\,\frac{\omega_e}{\tau_e}, \qquad F_{\mathrm{fert},N} = \frac{\Delta M_N}{\Delta t}, \qquad F_{\mathrm{fert},P} = \frac{1000\,\Delta M_P}{\Delta t}, \]

where \(\omega_e\) is the exact temporal intersection of \([t, t + \Delta t]\) with event window \(e\), guaranteeing cumulative mass invariance under arbitrary time-step refinement.

Managed biomass removal, livestock grazing, fire, and tillage

  1. Harvest export and residue partitioning (HarvestManagement, Level 3): partitions harvested aboveground biomass into exported economic yield \(F_{\mathrm{harv}} = f_{\mathrm{rem}} B_{\mathrm{ag}} / \Delta t\), surface residue return \(F_{\mathrm{res}} = (1 - f_{\mathrm{rem}}) B_{\mathrm{ag}} / \Delta t\), carbon export \(F_{C,\mathrm{exp}} = f_C F_{\mathrm{harv}}\), and associated root mortality without losing biomass to unlogged clipping.

  2. Linear body-mass livestock grazing (GrazingManagement, Level 3): evaluates daily areal dry-matter intake demand \(I_{\mathrm{area}}\) [kg m\(^{-2}\) day\(^{-1}\)] linearly in animal body weight \(W\) [kg] with daily intake percentage \(a\) [% body weight day\(^{-1}\)], forage digestibility/quality \(Q\), selectivity \(s_Q\), Hodgson (1990) asymptotic sward availability \(1 - \exp(-k\cdot 10^4 B_{\mathrm{ag}})\), and stocking density \(D\) [animals ha\(^{-1}\)]:

    \[I_{\mathrm{area}} = \frac{W\,a}{100}\,Q\,s_Q\left(1 - \exp\!\left(-10^4 k B_{\mathrm{ag}}\right)\right)\frac{D}{10^4}, \qquad F_{\mathrm{rem}} = \frac{\min\!\left(I_{\mathrm{area}},\; U_{\max} B_{\mathrm{ag}},\; B_{\mathrm{ag}}\,t_{\mathrm{day}}/\Delta t\right)}{t_{\mathrm{day}}}.\]

    Ingested forage carbon and nitrogen partition across livestock retention/export, dung return, and urine return via stoichiometric incidence \(N_h \mathbf{r}_h\) so \(F_{C,\mathrm{in}} = F_{C,\mathrm{dung}} + F_{C,\mathrm{exp}}\) and \(F_{N,\mathrm{in}} = F_{N,\mathrm{dung}} + F_{N,\mathrm{urine}} + F_{N,\mathrm{exp}}\) close explicitly alongside moisture-sensitive trampling damage.

  3. Stoichiometric fire disturbance (FireManagement, Level 3): combusts fuel load \(B_{\mathrm{burn}} = f_{\mathrm{burn}} B_{\mathrm{fuel}}\) and maps burned dry matter through species emission factors \(\mathrm{EF}_s\) [g kg\(^{-1}\)] (\(F_s = 10^{-3} B_{\mathrm{burn}}\,\mathrm{EF}_s / t_{\mathrm{day}}\)), ash deposition, and unburned mortality residue within one closed Level 3 reaction-port ledger.

  4. Tillage disturbance and empirical CO2 pulse (TillageManagement, Level 2): evaluates exponential post-tillage decay \(\exp(-k_{\mathrm{dec}} t_{\mathrm{since}})\), moisture-modulated residue incorporation fraction \(R_{\mathrm{inc}} \in [0, 1]\), and the phenomenological Reicosky (1997) soil \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) efflux pulse (REPLACE) without directly mutating pedosphere carbon pools.
